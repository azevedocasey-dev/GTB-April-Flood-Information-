About this dashboard
Built by an independent researcher and volunteer, in solidarity with the Grand Traverse Band of Ottawa and Chippewa Indians to support capacity, coordination, and responsible information sharing during the April 2026 flood emergency. I am a PhD (in progress) researcher focused on river restoration and community-based environmental work. Through my ongoing collaboration in this watershed and with GTB’s Natural Resource Department staff, I felt a responsibility to contribute in a practical way during the flood response. This site is one small effort to support information sharing and coordination using publicly available data. This is not an official GTB government publication — it is a research and coordination tool made freely available to support the Tribe's response and recovery work. All framing reflects my interpretation of GTB's own stated mission, goals, and values. This site references the six-county service area commonly used for coordination and public information. These boundaries are included for practical purposes and do not reflect the full scope of the GTB’s relationships to land, water, and stewardship. Any errors or omissions are my own. Updates, corrections, or resources to share are welcome. eaacasey@gmail.com

2025 goals — flood recovery alignment
Natural Resources & Environment
Protect and restore the natural world for future generations
Fish Pass restoration demonstrably reduced flood damage. Spring steelhead and walleye runs active in flood-affected reaches — 1836 treaty rights provide legal standing to advocate for priority protection of spawning reaches. GTB's water quality monitoring provides baseline data for impact comparison.
Dedibinezowin — Sovereignty & Treaty Rights
Preserve, strengthen, and assert tribal sovereignty
FEMA direct applicant path is an assertion of sovereignty — GTB defines what gets repaired, how, and on what timeline. Government-to-government coordination with FEMA Region 5 and county emergency managers is required. 1836 Treaty rights protect treaty fishery resource claims.
Kina Kitigaan — Food Sovereignty
Strengthen food sovereignty through traditional practices
Ziigwan is the core food sovereignty season — ramps, fish runs, sugarbush, planting. Flooding has directly impacted gathering areas and treaty fisheries during the most critical harvest window. Document food access impacts for FEMA agricultural assistance eligibility.
Naadiziwin — Culture & Language
Preserve, restore, and practice Anishinaabemowin
Mashkiigaki — "the swamp place" — the name encodes the original ecological identity of this restored wetland. Consult THPO and language keepers as part of damage assessment. This flood event is history — document it from the tribal perspective including the Fish Pass resilience story.
Public Safety & Health
Exercise public safety consistent with sovereign authority
Health clinic access — confirm alternate routes and activate Regroup mass notification. Elder housing in low-lying areas is priority — cross-reference elder services routes against road closure map. Six-county health accessibility is a specific Tribal Council goal.
Financial Management
Efficient, effective fiduciary management
Track all emergency expenditures with receipts from the start — FEMA Category B reimbursable. NOAA grant compliance — document all Lower River site impacts. Post-disaster HMGP window is now open for mitigation grant applications for Crystal crossings and Boardman infrastructure upgrades.

Priority environmental concerns — strengths-based
Fisheries · Boardman River
🔴 Fish Pass & spawning reaches
Active NOAA restoration site. Spring steelhead and walleye runs underway in flood-affected reaches. High turbidity and debris affect fish passage and spawning gravels.
Strength: Recent restoration work likely reduced flood damage — documented example that ecological restoration and flood resilience are the same investment. GTB's 1836 treaty rights provide legal standing to advocate for priority protection of spawning reaches.
Watch: Document all impacts before cleanup for NOAA grant reporting. Monitor turbidity and debris in spawning gravels.
Infrastructure · Crystal River
🔴 Stream crossings 1–4
Four fish passage structures — all in active flood conditions. High flow can shift culverts, scour abutments, and block fish passage.
Strength: Newly improved structures designed for higher flows. Damage documentation now protects grant warranty claims and FEMA Category C eligibility.
Watch: Structural check before any vehicle traffic. Debris at inlets and scour around abutments.
Food sovereignty · Traditional harvest
🔴 Spring gathering areas
Ramps (zhiibiins/wild leeks), fiddleheads, and spring greens at peak harvest in floodplain areas most impacted by flooding.
Strength: Traditional knowledge of plant locations and flood patterns supports rapid assessment by community members and natural resource staff.
Watch: Assess contamination risk before harvest resumes. Document gathering area damage as cultural resource impacts.
Wetland restoration · Emmet County
🟡 Mashkiigaki
Restored wetland (former Timber Shores Campground). Mashkiigaki means "the swamp place" in Anishinaabemowin — the name encodes this land's original ecological identity.
Strength: Wetland restoration aligned with the land's original character. Wetlands absorb flood energy and protect downstream areas.
Watch: Invasive species seed dispersal via floodwaters. Debris in restored areas.
Water quality · Great Lakes watershed
🟡 Tributaries to Grand Traverse Bay
GTB territory drains to Grand Traverse Bay and Lake Michigan. Flood runoff carries nutrients, pathogens, and contaminants into tribal waters and the Great Lakes.
Strength: GTB's existing water quality monitoring provides baseline data for impact comparison and documentation for EGLE and USEPA coordination.
Watch: E. coli and turbidity in key reaches. Report anomalies to EGLE and USEPA Region 5.
Cultural heritage · All sites
🟡 Sacred sites & cultural areas
Assess condition of sacred sites, burial grounds, and ceremonial areas in the flood extent. Flooding can expose or damage culturally significant places.
Strength: GTB's THPO has formal role in recovery — NHPA Section 106 consultation is both a legal protection and an exercise of sovereignty.
Watch: Consult THPO before any ground-disturbing recovery work near sensitive areas.

A note on this document and its language GTB's 2023 Natural Hazard Mitigation Plan is a FEMA-required instrument, written in FEMA's framework and approved under FEMA's criteria. It uses language like "vulnerability" and organizes GTB's territory by county boundaries — both of which reflect the federal planning framework rather than how the tribe describes itself or relates to its land and water. The plan is referenced here as a legal and financial asset that unlocks recovery funding and establishes GTB's eligibility as a direct applicant. It is not a description of who GTB is. GTB's own account of its relationship to this land, these waterways, and this community is expressed in the Tribal Council's goals, the natural resources department's restoration work, and the tribe's ongoing exercise of treaty rights. Those are the primary frame for this dashboard. Where specific entries in the Safety or Science tabs draw on the MHP for technical information — evacuation protocols, infrastructure assessments, mitigation actions — the relevant section is noted as a source.

GTB controls project worksheets, procurement, and reporting — including how culturally and ecologically sensitive restoration sites are assessed and repaired.
